\begin{abstract}
In SemEval-2026 Task~9, a text is polarized exactly when at least one social
dimension applies, yet shared-task systems predict the two subtasks
separately and repair contradictions after decoding. We report the first
stage of a controlled study of this hierarchy on English. Five conditions
share one DeBERTa-v3-base encoder: independent models (M1), a multi-task model
(M2), post-hoc gating of M2 (M3), and M4-core, which derives detection from the
dimension probabilities through a differentiable noisy-OR. Over
5~folds~$\times$~5~seeds (100 training runs), with paired bootstrap
confidence intervals, the unconstrained models contradict the hierarchy on
62\% of all texts, while M3 and M4-core reach zero violations. Under our
pre-registered metrics M4-core does \emph{not} improve macro-F1 over M1 or M2
(RQ1): \textsc{type} macro-F1 is $-$0.082 and $-$0.036 lower. At equal
consistency it is statistically tied with gating (RQ2: $+$0.008, 95\% CI
[$-$0.009, $+$0.022]), while recovering more labels on multi-label texts
($+$0.025). A gating audit shows that M3 deletes 23\% of the multi-task
model's correct dimension labels, because 98.7\% of its detection misses
already carried a correct dimension. We trace M4-core's detection deficit to a
decoding rule that tunes dimension thresholds without seeing non-polarized
text, and show that the \textsc{type} ranking reverses when all texts are
scored.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Polarized language carries hostility, stereotyping, exclusion, or divisive
framing. The signals are contextual rather than lexical: political vocabulary
alone does not make a text polarized, and one text can involve several social
dimensions at once. SemEval-2026 Task~9 \citep{naseem-etal-2026-polar}
splits the problem into \textsc{PolarDetect} (\DET{}: is the text polarized?)
and \textsc{PolarType} (\TYPE{}: which of Political, Racial/ethnic,
Religious, Gender/sexual identity, and Other apply?).

The two subtasks are \emph{nested}, not merely related. \TYPE{} labels exist
only for polarized texts, so a text is polarized if and only if some dimension
applies. Standard multi-task learning puts independent heads on a shared
encoder, so nothing represents this constraint. \citet{yeze-2026} note that
independent modelling ``causes hierarchical violations''.
\citet{sagarmatha-2026} measure violation rates of up to 33\% and remove them
with a deterministic rule at inference. They report that the rule ``slightly
limits raw multi-label recall'': on English, gating lifts \DET{} from 0.752 to
0.826 but lowers \TYPE{} from 0.511 to 0.506.

Every published fix acts on outputs. We ask whether the recall cost belongs
to the constraint itself or to the point at which it is applied. Our
structured model, M4-core, has no detection head. It computes the detection
probability from the five dimension probabilities through a noisy-OR, so
contradictory outputs cannot be represented. On non-polarized text, the
detection loss reaches the dimension detectors, which is exactly where \TYPE{}
supervision is missing.

This report covers the mid-submission scope of the project. It addresses two
contributions:
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item \textbf{C1 / RQ1.} Does deriving \DET{} from \TYPE{} via noisy-OR
improve \DET{} and/or \TYPE{} macro-F1 over independent models (M1) and
unconstrained multi-task learning (M2)?
\item \textbf{C2 / RQ2.} At equal hard-prediction consistency, does
training-time structure (M4-core) preserve more \TYPE{} recall and F1 than
post-hoc deterministic reconciliation (M3)?
\end{itemize}
Label-aware representations (C3/RQ3) and the parameter-efficiency comparison
with 12B--27B systems (C4/RQ4) are scheduled after the mid-submission
(\S\ref{sec:remaining}).

\paragraph{Summary of findings.}
Unconstrained models violate the hierarchy on 62\% of texts, while M3 and
M4-core reach 0\%. RQ1 is \emph{not supported}: M4-core is below both
baselines. RQ2 is \emph{inconclusive}: M4-core ties with gating on macro
metrics, but is better on multi-label texts and more stable across seeds. A
gating audit quantifies the error propagation behind M3's recall loss. Two
protocol choices shape the RQ1 outcome: \TYPE{} is scored only on polarized
texts, and M4-core's thresholds are tuned without seeing non-polarized text.
Both are documented here and are the first targets of the next stage.

\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{The POLAR benchmark.}
SemEval-2026 Task~9 covers 22 languages and attracted 67 teams and 73 system
papers \citep{naseem-etal-2026-polar}. Two properties of the English data
shaped our design. First, annotator agreement is only fair (Fleiss's
$\kappa=0.39$, against 0.83 for Khmer), so part of the label signal is
genuinely contested. Second, headroom differs sharply between subtasks. The
best English \DET{} score is 0.825 against a 0.773 baseline, whereas \TYPE{}
reaches 0.532 against 0.347. Detection is close to saturated; dimension
classification is not.

\paragraph{POLAR systems.}
Large generative models dominated the task: Qwen was used by 31\% of teams,
LLaMA by 20\%, and Gemma/Gemini by 19\%, against about 7\% for BERT-style
encoders. Both English subtask winners have 12B--27B parameters
\citep{naseem-etal-2026-polar,utokyo-2026,nycu-2026}. Among encoder systems,
\citet{daniyal-etal-2026-msqrd} compare BERT variants, \citet{tran-2026-uit}
add an LLM refinement stage to DeBERTa, \citet{orsanigo-etal-2026-digis}
report multi-task gains, and \citet{nestor-etal-2026-polarizedteam} use
label-wise attention. Our protocol follows \citet{smash-2026}:
cross-validation with out-of-fold threshold calibration.

\paragraph{Hierarchy and consistency.}
Almost every team tunes per-label thresholds \citep{naseem-etal-2026-polar},
so we treat that as standard practice. \citet{yeze-2026} observe
violations but leave their measurement and correction to future work.
\citet{nycu-2026} check the \DET{} prediction before assigning \TYPE{} labels.
\citet{polafusion-2026} route inputs through a binary gatekeeper that zeroes
child labels for non-polarized text, and force an argmax when a polarized
text fires nothing. \citet{sagarmatha-2026} formalise the Logical Violation
Rate (LVR), audit it across all languages, enforce
$\hat{y}_{\text{S1}}=0 \Rightarrow \hat{y}_{\text{S2}}=0$ at inference, and
feed the child task \emph{detached} parent logits. That stop-gradient
deliberately prevents the tasks from shaping each other's representations.

\paragraph{The gap.}
In every system, the constraint acts on outputs: detection remains an
independent prediction that is overruled later. We found no Task~9 system in
which detection is parameterised as a function of the dimension
predictions. Hierarchy-consistent neural classifiers are established in
structured prediction \citep{giunchiglia2020}, multi-task learning has a long
history \citep{caruana1997,kendall2018}, and label-wise attention goes back to
\citet{mullenbach2018}. None of these ideas has been applied to POLAR.

\paragraph{Inputs and outputs.}
The input is one social-media text, tokenised with the DeBERTa-v3 tokenizer
and truncated to 256 tokens. Every model outputs a \DET{} decision
$\hat{d}\in\{0,1\}$ and five \TYPE{} decisions
$\hat{\mathbf{t}}\in\{0,1\}^5$, together with the underlying probabilities.

\paragraph{Data.}
We use the official English data: 3{,}222 training, 160 development and
1{,}452 test texts \citep{naseem-etal-2026-polar}. Table~\ref{tab:data}
summarises the training split. The gold labels satisfy the hierarchy exactly
in all three files. \textit{Other} co-occurs with named dimensions in 121
training texts, so we model it as an ordinary fifth label rather than an
exclusive residual. The supplied test file contains label columns whose
provenance is unconfirmed. Until that is resolved, we treat it as strictly
held out and do not use it. The 160-text development set has single-digit
positive counts for the rare dimensions, so it cannot support ablation claims
\citep{smash-2026}. All conclusions therefore rest on out-of-fold predictions
over the 3{,}222 training texts.

All conditions share \textbf{DeBERTa-v3-base} \citep{he2023debertav3}, which
has 184M parameters including embeddings. Each uses the attention-masked mean
of the final hidden states, dropout 0.1 and linear heads
(Figure~\ref{fig:models}). Let $z_k$ be the logit of dimension $k$,
$p_k=\sigma(z_k)$, and $\pos$ the gold-polarized examples in a batch. The
\TYPE{} loss is masked to $\pos$ in every trained model, because \TYPE{}
labels are undefined elsewhere \citep{polafusion-2026}. It uses per-label
positive weights $w_k=\#\text{neg}_k/\#\text{pos}_k$, computed within $\pos$.

\paragraph{M1 (independent).}
Two separately fine-tuned encoders, as in \citet{yeze-2026}: M1-\DET{} with
binary cross-entropy (BCE), and M1-\TYPE{} with masked weighted BCE. Their
predictions are joined without reconciliation. M1 has twice the parameters
(368M).

\paragraph{M2 (shared multi-task).}
One encoder with a \DET{} head and a \TYPE{} head:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} = \mathrm{BCE}(y_{\text{det}}, p_{\text{det}}) + \lambda \cdot
\frac{1}{|\pos|}\sum_{i\in\pos}\mathcal{L}^{(i)}_{\text{type}},
\label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
with $\lambda=1$. Nothing ties the heads together, so M2 can violate the
hierarchy in either direction.

\paragraph{M3 (post-hoc reconciliation).}
M3 is derived from M2's thresholded predictions $\hat d$, $\hat t_k$, reusing
M2's frozen thresholds. This is our re-implementation of the deterministic
constraint of \citet{sagarmatha-2026}. The \emph{one-way} variant sets
$\hat t_k \leftarrow \hat t_k \wedge \hat d$, which removes
non-polarized-with-type violations only. The \emph{symmetric} variant then
sets $\hat d \leftarrow \bigvee_k \hat t_k$, which also removes
polarized-without-type violations, so $\LVR=0$ is guaranteed. We keep the raw
and reconciled predictions to audit what gating removes.

\paragraph{M4-core (structured noisy-OR).}
The same encoder and \TYPE{} head as M2, with \emph{no} \DET{} head:
\begin{equation}
p_{\text{det}} = 1-\prod_{k=1}^{5}(1-p_k),
\label{eq:noisyor}
\end{equation}
trained with the loss of Eq.~\ref{eq:loss}. We compute Eq.~\ref{eq:noisyor} in
log space: with $s=\sum_k \mathrm{softplus}(z_k)$, the logit is
$\log(e^{s}-1)$, evaluated with two numerically stable branches. On a
non-polarized text the \DET{} loss pushes \emph{every} $z_k$ down, which gives
the dimension detectors supervision exactly where masking removes it. At
inference, $\hat t_k = \mathbf{1}[p_k \ge \tau_k]$ and
$\hat d = \bigvee_k \hat t_k$, so binary $\LVR=0$ holds by construction.
M4-core omits the label-aware attention of our proposal on purpose. The
mid-submission comparison changes one mechanism at a time, so any M3-versus-M4
difference can be attributed to the hierarchy rather than to the head
architecture.

\section{Experimental Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

\paragraph{Cross-validation.}
We create one fixed five-fold split with iterative multilabel stratification
over \DET{} and the five \TYPE{} labels \citep{sechidis2011}, using seed 2026.
The folds hold 644--645 texts each, with rare labels balanced (14--15 Gender,
22--23 Religious per fold). The split is reused for every model and every
seed (13, 21, 42, 87, 100). Within each outer split, 10\% of the four
training folds (258 texts) forms an inner validation set, stratified the same
way. Early stopping, checkpoint selection and all thresholds use only this
inner set. The outer fold is predicted exactly once, with the selected state
and thresholds frozen. This yields 4 trained conditions $\times$ 5 folds
$\times$ 5 seeds $=$ \textbf{100 training runs}. M1 is joined, and both M3
variants are derived, for all 25 fold--seed pairs.

\paragraph{Training.}
AdamW \citep{loshchilov2019}, learning rate $2\times10^{-5}$, weight decay
0.01, batch size 8 with 2 accumulation steps, linear schedule with 10\%
warm-up, gradient clipping at 1.0, FP16 mixed precision, at most 8 epochs,
early-stopping patience 2. The checkpoint is selected on inner \DET{}
macro-F1 for M1-\DET{} and on inner \TYPE{} macro-F1 otherwise.

\paragraph{Thresholds.}
All thresholds are searched over $\{0.20,0.25,\dots,0.80\}$. Each \TYPE{}
threshold $\tau_k$ maximises that label's F1 on the inner-validation polarized
texts. M1-\DET{} and M2 select a \DET{} threshold that maximises \DET{}
macro-F1. M3 inherits M2's thresholds, and M4-core has no \DET{} threshold
(Eq.~\ref{eq:noisyor} plus OR decoding).

\paragraph{Metrics.}
\DET{} macro-F1 averages F1 over both classes. \TYPE{} macro-P/R/F1 average
over the five labels and, as frozen in our plan, are computed on
\emph{gold-polarized} texts. We additionally report \TYPE{} on \emph{all}
texts as a pre-declared secondary view (\S\ref{sec:scope}). The symmetric
Logical Violation Rate, extended from \citet{sagarmatha-2026} to both
directions, is
\begin{equation}
\LVR = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{1}\big[\hat d^{(i)} \neq
\textstyle\bigvee_k \hat t^{(i)}_k\big],
\end{equation}
split into LVR-A ($\hat d=0$ with some $\hat t_k=1$) and LVR-B ($\hat d=1$
with no $\hat t_k=1$). We also report label-level recall on multi-label and
single-label polarized texts.

\paragraph{Aggregation and statistics.}
For each seed, the five outer folds are concatenated into one 3{,}222-text
prediction set and scored once. Tables show the mean $\pm$ standard deviation
of the five seed-level scores; we never treat the 25 fold--seed scores as
independent. Paired comparisons use an example-level paired bootstrap
\citep{koehn2004} with 2{,}000 resamples. Each resample applies the same
multiset of texts to both models and all five seeds. The per-seed difference
is averaged over seeds, and the 2.5/97.5 percentiles form the 95\% CI. The
six pre-declared primary tests (Table~\ref{tab:primary}) are Holm-corrected
\citep{holm1979}. We also report how many of the five seeds favour each
model. Secondary comparisons are exploratory and uncorrected.

\paragraph{Compute.}
All 100 runs ran on one NVIDIA RTX 4060 Laptop GPU (8\,GB; peak memory
4.5\,GB) with PyTorch 2.5.1 and Transformers 4.57.6. Each run took 1.9--2.9
minutes, 3.9 GPU-hours in total (Appendix~\ref{app:training}).

\subsection{Hierarchy consistency}
\label{sec:lvr}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig_lvr.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Hierarchy violations.} Each bar is the share of all 3{,}222
texts whose hard predictions contradict the hierarchy, averaged over seeds.
Purple is LVR-A (``not polarized'' with a dimension); green is LVR-B
(``polarized'' with no dimension). M1 and M2 contradict themselves on 62\% of
texts, almost entirely in direction A. M3 and M4-core never do.}
\label{fig:lvr}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:lvr} shows that the unconstrained models violate the
hierarchy on \textbf{61.9\%} (M1) and \textbf{61.6\%} (M2) of all texts,
nearly twice the highest rate \citet{sagarmatha-2026} report. Almost all of
it is LVR-A. M2 attaches a dimension to every one of the 2{,}047
non-polarized texts, and M1 to 2{,}039 of them (Table~\ref{tab:det}).

This is a direct consequence of how the \TYPE{} heads are trained. The masked
loss means they never see a non-polarized text, the positive weights push
their probabilities up, and their thresholds are tuned only on polarized
texts. Nothing teaches them what non-polarized text looks like.

M3 (both variants) and M4-core reach exactly 0\% in every run. That confirms
the invariants of \S\ref{sec:models}: symmetric M3 and M4-core have
$\LVR=0$, and one-way M3 has LVR-A $=0$. The interesting question is
therefore not whether consistency is reached, but what it costs, which is
RQ2.

\paragraph{The \TYPE{} gap is the price of consistency.}
Because \TYPE{} is scored only on gold-polarized texts, a model is never
penalised for dimensions it predicts on non-polarized texts. M1 and M2
exploit this fully (\S\ref{sec:lvr}). M4-core, like M3, can only be right on
a polarized text if its detection also fires, so its \TYPE{} recall is capped
by its detection recall. The RQ1 \TYPE{} comparison therefore pits a
consistent model against inconsistent ones. The fair comparison at matched
consistency is RQ2, and when the same predictions are scored on all texts the
sign reverses (\S\ref{sec:scope}). Restricted to polarized texts, M4-core is
still \emph{more precise} than M2 ($+$0.021 \TYPE{} macro-P, CI

\paragraph{The \DET{} gap is a decoding artifact.}
M4-core over-detects (Table~\ref{tab:det}): 472 false positives per seed
against 345 for M2, and 199 misses against 281. Its \DET{} recall is 0.831
against 0.761 for M2 (+0.070, CI [$+$0.059, $+$0.081]), while precision falls
from 0.722 to 0.674.

The decoding rule explains this. M4-core's \DET{} decision is the OR of five
thresholds, each tuned for \emph{its own label's} F1 on polarized texts only,
so no tuning objective ever sees the cost of a false alarm on a non-polarized
text. The Political threshold, which effectively decides detection, is
driven to the bottom of the grid: mean 0.22, and exactly 0.20 in 19 of 25
runs (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:thresholds}). M1 and M2 instead tune a dedicated
\DET{} threshold for \DET{} macro-F1, and M1-\DET{} also selects its
checkpoint on \DET{}.

The \DET{} comparison is therefore not decoding-matched. We report this as a
property of the frozen protocol rather than adjusting it after seeing results.

\paragraph{Sharing alone also hurts.}
M2 is below M1 by $-$0.046 \TYPE{} F1 (CI [$-$0.060, $-$0.033]) and $-$0.006
\DET{} F1. This is consistent with the negative transfer on sparse labels
reported by \citet{yeze-2026}. However, M1 uses twice the parameters and a
\DET{}-selected checkpoint.

\subsection{RQ2: post-hoc gating versus training-time structure}
\label{sec:rq2}

With both M3 and M4-core at $\LVR=0$, the primary comparisons are
statistically null (Table~\ref{tab:primary}). M4-core's \TYPE{} F1 is
$+$0.008 higher (CI [$-$0.009, $+$0.022], 3/5 seeds) and its \TYPE{} recall
$+$0.006 (CI [$-$0.014, $+$0.023], 2/5 seeds), with $p_{\text{Holm}}=0.73$
for both. \textbf{RQ2 is inconclusive.} The data support neither ``structure
preserves more recall than gating'' nor the reverse, and we report it as
such.

Three secondary results qualify this:
\begin{enumerate}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item On the 426 \textbf{multi-label} texts, M4-core recovers more gold labels
than M3: label recall 0.610 vs.\ 0.585, $+$0.025 (CI [$+$0.009, $+$0.041],
4/5 seeds; Table~\ref{tab:secondary}). This is the direction
\citet{sagarmatha-2026} anticipate (``gating limits multi-label recall''),
but it is an exploratory comparison.
\item M4-core is \textbf{more stable}: its \TYPE{} F1 seed sd is 0.007,
against 0.022 for M3.
\item M4-core pays $-$0.008 \DET{} macro-F1 (CI [$-$0.016, $-$0.001]) for its
broader detection.
\end{enumerate}

labels because the parent decision was wrong. The symmetric OR step almost
never fires (0.4 \DET{} changes per seed).

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the evidence supports.}
The diagnostic part of C2 holds on its own. Unconstrained heads trained with
a masked \TYPE{} loss are almost maximally inconsistent (62\% of texts,
essentially every non-polarized one). Post-hoc gating fixes this at a
quantifiable price: 23\% of correct labels, removed almost exclusively through
detection misses. Training-time structure reaches the same consistency with
equal macro accuracy, better multi-label recall and lower variance. The
evidence does \emph{not} support the claims that noisy-OR improves macro-F1
over independent or multi-task models (RQ1), or that structure preserves
more \TYPE{} recall than gating in the macro average (RQ2). Following our
plan, we do not reframe either question after seeing the results.

\paragraph{Why the trade-off did not disappear.}
Our proposal hypothesised that M4 avoids gating's recall loss because it
``discards nothing''. The audit shows why that is incomplete: under
\emph{any} consistent decoder, a polarized text whose detection fails yields
no dimension, so \TYPE{} recall on polarized texts is bounded by \DET{}
recall. M4-core raises \DET{} recall (0.831 vs.\ 0.761), which is where its
Political and Gender gains come from, but pays in \DET{} precision because
its per-label thresholds never see non-polarized text. The binding constraint
is the \emph{decoder}, not the training objective, which makes a
\DET{}-aware threshold search the most informative next experiment.

\section{Conclusion}

We built and ran a controlled, fully paired comparison of four ways to
handle the POLAR detection/dimension hierarchy: 100 training runs, 25 derived
conditions per post-processed model, and bootstrap CIs with multiplicity
control.
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item Unconstrained models contradict the hierarchy on most texts.
\item Post-hoc gating removes the contradictions but also nearly a quarter of
the correct dimension labels.
\item A noisy-OR model reaches the same consistency with statistically equal
macro accuracy, better multi-label recall and lower variance, but it does not
beat the unconstrained baselines on the frozen metric.
\end{itemize}
Whether structure can \emph{eliminate} the consistency--recall trade-off now
hinges on its decoder, which the next stage addresses.

% ===================================================================
\section*{Limitations}

\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item \textbf{Scoring scope.} \TYPE{} is scored on gold-polarized texts, as
frozen before the experiments. This favours unconstrained models. We have not
yet confirmed how the official scorer treats non-polarized texts.
\item \textbf{Decoding not matched.} M1 and M2 tune a \DET{} threshold for
\DET{}; M4-core cannot. Checkpoint selection also differs (M1-\DET{} on
\DET{}, others on \TYPE{}). The Political threshold of M4-core hits the grid
floor in 19/25 runs.
\item \textbf{Parameter mismatch.} M1 uses 368M parameters against 184M for
the other conditions.
\item \textbf{Uncertainty.} Bootstrap CIs capture sampling variation over
texts. Training randomness is reflected only through five seeds. Only the six
primary tests are multiplicity-corrected.
\item \textbf{Scope.} The results cover English cross-validation only.
Weights were not retained in the sweep, so the development and test sets are
not yet scored, and absolute numbers are not leaderboard-comparable.
\item \textbf{Training budget.} Training is capped at 8 epochs, and 3 of 100
runs selected the final epoch.
\item \textbf{Hardware.} The runs used one consumer GPU rather than the
planned cluster. Every condition used the same machine, so the comparison is
internally consistent.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Ethical Considerations}
We use the released POLAR data without collecting new data. The texts contain
hostile and stereotyping content about protected groups. Polarization
classifiers can be misused for surveillance or for silencing legitimate
political speech, and their errors fall unevenly on the rare dimensions,
where our models are weakest (Gender/sexual F1 $\leq$ 0.27). We report
per-label results so that these weaknesses stay visible.
